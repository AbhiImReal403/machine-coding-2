## 1. The Qualities of a Good Customer Service Executive

A good customer service executive should be:

* **Soft-spoken**, **gently persuasive**, **encouraging**, **attentive**, **pleasant**, and **well-mannered**.
* **A good listener** – let the customer talk and respond appropriately.
* **Well-informed** about the product, company policies, and empowered to make decisions.
* **Politely firm**, **clear in speech**, **calm**, **patient**, and **assertive**.
* **Empathetic** and **objective** – focus on problem-solving.
* **Takes initiative**, **owns the problem**, and works toward customer satisfaction.
* **Sincere**, **authentic**, and **willing to advocate for the customer**.

## 2. Organising a Portfolio

* A **powerful tool** to provide **evidence of skills and accomplishments**.
* **Benefits**:

  * Aids **interview preparation** and **self-assessment**.
  * **Demonstrates** skills and career progress.
* **Contents**:

  * Articles, certificates, references, photos, transcripts, programming samples, etc.
* **Format**:

  * **Sturdy 3-ring binder**, **20 pages max**, neat and organized.
* **Structure**:

  1. **Index**
  2. **Resume/CV**
  3. Certificates, commendations
  4. Education and achievements
  5. Other items in **chronological order**
* **Web version**: Adds a tech-savvy, accessible, and visually appealing option.
* **Keep it updated** and **tailored to each role**.

## 3. A Creative Approach to Job Seeking

* Go **beyond traditional job ads**.
* **Do research**: Read business news to find growing companies.
* **Network**: Build and maintain contacts; follow up regularly.
* **Targeted applications**:

  * Personalize each application.
  * Call before sending a letter.
  * Clearly state job interest and demonstrate company knowledge.
  * Use persuasive tone.
  * Follow up with a phone call to express commitment.

## 4. Gender and the Workplace

* **Sex differences persist**, though roles are evolving.
* **Hiring**:

  * Efforts for **equal opportunities** regardless of gender.
* **Challenges for Women**:

  * **Wage gap**, **dual roles**, **security concerns**, **discrimination**, **harassment**, and **pregnancy-related bias**.
* **Organizational Solutions**:

  * **Equal policies**, **maternity leave**, **gender sensitization**, and **supportive rules**.

## 5. Workplace Etiquette

* A set of **polite behaviors** that foster a positive work environment.

### Professional Appearance:

* **Grooming**, **hygiene**, **dress code**, **pleasant expression**.

### Behavior Code:

* Greet, thank, speak softly, **listen actively**, **respect opinions**.
* Keep workspace clean, **avoid gossip**, use phones and internet responsibly.
* **Avoid personal conversations**, inappropriate behavior, or distractions.

## 6. Work Ethics

Defined as a **set of moral principles** guiding professional conduct.

### Core Characteristics:

* **Interpersonal Skills**: Behavior and attitudes affecting interaction.
* **Initiative**: Taking charge without supervision.
* **Dependability**: Being reliable, punctual, and honest.

### Employer Expectations:

* Good communication, positive attitude, self-confidence, and responsibility.

### Values:

* **Traditional**: Trust, honesty, loyalty.
* **Modern**: Innovation, teamwork, and improvement.

## 7. Portfolio vs Resume

| Feature      | Resume                       | Portfolio                                    |
| ------------ | ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Nature       | Concise document             | Comprehensive collection                     |
| Contents     | Credentials, education, jobs | Resume + certificates, work samples, designs |
| Length       | 1–2 pages                    | Up to 20 pages                               |
| Usage        | Sent in applications         | Brought to interviews                        |
| Purpose      | Brief overview               | Tangible proof of ability                    |
| Update Style | Infrequently                 | Regularly updated, job-specific              |

## 8. Stages of Presentation

### 1. Beginning

* Greet audience.
* State topic.
* Define new terms.
* Set expectations for questions.
* Outline structure.

### 2. Body

* Use **clear sequencing** of ideas.
* Present **main idea → explanation → result**.
* Transition smoothly between topics.

### 3. Conclusion

* **Restate purpose**.
* **Summarize key points**.
* Use phrases like:

  * “So can we agree on...”
  * “Finally, we conclude...”

## 9. Essentials of Good Customer Service

### Importance:

* Encourages **repeat business** and **positive word-of-mouth**.
* **Cost-effective** to retain existing customers.
* Shapes business **reputation and growth**.

### Best Practices:

* Answer phones promptly.
* Keep promises.
* Listen and resolve complaints.
* Help without expecting immediate gain.
* Offer something extra.

### "Moments of Truth":

* Any customer interaction is a chance to **impress or disappoint**.

### Managing "Moments of Truth":

* Be informed, empowered, empathetic.
* Own the problem, resolve issues calmly.
* **Under-promise, over-deliver**.
